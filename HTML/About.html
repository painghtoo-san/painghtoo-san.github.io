2006: Messi's first steps on the big stage

Messi's FIFA World Cup journey started in 2006. Still carrying the label of Argentina's new wonderkid, Messi was included in the 23-man squad of José Pékerman for the tournament in Germany. After watching the opening match from the bench, Messi got his first playing time, first assist and first goal in a World Cup, in the 6-0 win over Serbia and Montenegro. Taking a spot at the starting XI of the final group match against the Netherlands, Messi then had his first taste of World Cup knockout football on the day of his 19th birthday in Argentina's 2-1 extra-time win over Mexico. The Albiceleste's participation in the competition would end a few days later in a penalty shoot-out defeat against Germany, but for Messi, this was simply the beginning.

2010: Maradona and Messi

Without always reaching the heights they were capable of, Argentina qualified from the group stage of the 2010 tournament with three victories. Operating as more of a playmaker for the team, under legendary head coach Diego Maradona, Messi entered the knockout phase without having scored. In the round of 16, Messi helped the Albiceleste advance against Mexico, but then it was Germany that ended their hopes and dreams. The attacking talent that Argentina had at their disposal was not enough to prevent a heavy 4-0 defeat against Joachim Löw's men.

2014: Close, but no cigar for Argentina

Messi traveled to Brazil after an injury-hit season with FC Barcelona, fueling doubts about his form. From the very first fixture, though, he managed to show otherwise as a goal in the 2-1 win over Bosnia and Herzogovina was followed by a late strike against Iran and a double in a 3-2 victory against Nigeria. In the round of 16, Messi was the architect of Angel Di Maria's winner against Switzerland and a key factor of Argentina advancing beyond Belgium and the Netherlands in the quarter-finals and semi-finals, respectively. The final at the Maracana didn't have a happy ending in store. The Albiceleste suffered a painful defeat after Mario Götze's extra-time winner, although Messi still walked away with the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player.

2018: Uncomfortable early exit for Argentina

Four years after being one step away from winning the FIFA World Cup for a third time, Argentina struggled throughout their efforts in the 21st edition of the competition in Russia. Messi managed to score only once, a goal-of-the-tournament contender against Nigeria, in a nerve-racking 2-1 win at the final match of the group stage. That victory sent the Albiceleste into the round of 16, only to be eliminated by the eventual world champions France.

2022: A last shot at destiny

Argentina's path to the top of the FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022 was a road paved with hardship. Despite Messi scoring the opener, Argentina's shock 2-1 defeat against Saudi Arabia in the opening match of the group stage left the Albiceleste facing an unthinkable early exit. But consecutive 2-0 victories against Mexico and Poland put them back on track, with Messi breaking the deadlock against the Mexicans and Argentina then securing qualification against Lewandowski and co.

A leader throughout the knockouts

With the opening goal in the 2-1 win against Australia, Messi celebrated his 1000th career game by scoring his first-ever World Cup knockout goal. The quarter-final against the Netherlands was a nail-biter. Messi scored one and created another for Argentina, only for the Dutch to hit back with two late goals to take the game into extra-time and then penalties. In the shoot-out it was once again Messi that led the way for Argentina, with goalkeeper Emi Martinez helping his side progress 4-3. To top his world-class display against the Dutch, Messi had kept an even greater one up his sleeve for the semi-final. Against 2018 finalists Croatia, Messi's performance was another statement of intent. Not only did he score the opener but with a Messi-being-Messi moment created the third and final goal for Julián Álvarez with a sublime run and pass.
